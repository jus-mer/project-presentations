Multidimensional framework of meritocracy

To capture these different faces of meritocracy, we use a multidimensional framework that expands the usual way meritocracy is measured in empirical research.

Multidimensional framework of meritocracy

Multidimensional framework of meritocracy

What this model adds is, first, a distinction between perceptions and preferences, where perceptions refer to how people think society works and preferences refer to how they think it should work. This is represented in the upper-right corner using the same items, but with a normative sense.

Multidimensional framework of meritocracy

Second, it also incorporates non-meritocratic elements, captured through items on family wealth and social connections, both of which are shown at the bottom of the diagram. We call this dimension privilege.

Multidimensional framework of meritocracy

So, in total, the scale distinguishes four dimensions: perceived meritocracy, perceived privilege, preference for meritocracy, and acceptance of privilege. This means that recognizing privilege is not incompatible with supporting merit. Both can coexist within the same belief system.

Also, you can see the two items for each factor, all measured on a four-point agreement scale.

this was the first attempt to apply the multidimensional framework using ISSP 2009 data. We can see that the model’s fit indices are acceptable, so we can conclude that meritocracy can be measured using a multidimensional approach.

As a bit of context, this second part relates to my master’s thesis project, so I would really appreciate any comment or suggestion.

Let us observe the multidimensional framework of meritocracy again, composed of perceptions and preferences, on the one hand, and the distinction by meritocratic and privilege factors, on the other.

So we already know that people can hold meritocratic and privilege beliefs at the same time, but what we don’t know is how these different types of beliefs are organized in the people’s minds.

So, I move toward the use of methods to observe how meritocratic beliefs —such as the perception of effort— are organized in the population

By this I mean the following:

Latent Class Analysis

Let’s imagine that meritocratic beliefs are the brown ones and privilege beliefs are the red ones

Like I said before, we know that the coexistence of privilege and meritocratic beliefs is possible

but we still don’t know what beliefs predominate among people, and which ones are most representative at the population level

So, to find that out, I plan to use Latent Class Analysis, a method that allows us to observe the heterogenéity of beliefs in the population by classifying subgroups

By “heterogenéity,” I also mean differences in beliefs regarding the number of groups that can be identified and the size of each group in the population

LCA estimation

  • Parameters:

    • \(P_{jk}\): conditional probability of response \(j\) given class \(k\)
    • \(\pi_k\): estimated class proportion for class \(k\)

  • Model selection based on BIC, AIC, and entropy





Collins, L., & Lanza, S. (2010). Latent class and latent transition analysis: With applications in the social behavioral, and health sciences. London: Wiley.

So, using this method, we can identify two main parameters:

Ferst, the conditional probability of response for individuals within each class. This parameter allows us to provide a substantive interpretation of each latent group.

Second, the estimated proportion of the class, which tells us the size of each class within the population.

This modeling process is carried out using fit indices such as the Akaike Index Criterion, the Bayesian Index Criterion, and entropy, each of which provides information to identify the optimal model

Now I want to present you the preliminary results of the latent class estimation

  • Class 1 Critical Meritocrats (36.2%)

Urzúa, T., Castillo, J. C. (2026). Identifying Patterns of Beliefs Based on Merit and Privilege: A Latent Class Analysis Approach. Work in progress

So, starting with the f{e}rst class, it combines a low perception of meritocracy with a high perception of the influence of privilege. Its members believe that success does not depend on effort and talent, but rather on factors such as social background and networks. However, at the normative level, they strongly support meritocracy and reject the legítimacy of non-meritocratic advantages, especially those linked to personal connections.

the name of this class is critical meritocrats and has a size of 36% This group is cháracterized by its belief in meritocracy as a moral aideal, while acknowledging that, in reality, certain prívileges influence success. It does not consider the current system to be meritocratic, but it strongly ádvocats for it to be so

  • Class 2: Privilege advocates (36.3%)

Urzúa, T., Castillo, J. C. (2026). Identifying Patterns of Beliefs Based on Merit and Privilege: A Latent Class Analysis Approach. Work in progress

The second class follows the same descriptive framework as the first, perceiving low levels of meritocratic functions and high levels of privilege indicators. The main difference lies in the fact that this class accepts that society operates according to norms based on family background and social capital.

This class is called Privilege advocates and has a size of 36%, combines a realistic assessment of a stratified society with a normative justification of privilege. Thus, meritocracy is not presented as an alternative to privilege; rather, the two principles coexist and are viewed as compatible foundations for the functioning of society.

  • Class 3 Universal rewards (20.7%)

Urzúa, T., Castillo, J. C. (2026). Identifying Patterns of Beliefs Based on Merit and Privilege: A Latent Class Analysis Approach. Work in progress

Now, as we can see, people in the therd group recognize both meritocratic and privilege elements in society, which sets them apart from the previous two groups. And normatively, they strongly endorse meritocracy, while taking a more ambivalent stance toward privilege, but they still show support for non-meritocracy

  • This class is called Universal rewards and has a size of 21%. This profile reflects a belief system in which merit and privilege operate simultaneously. They strongly support meritocracy, though they hold an ambivalent stance toward privilege: they view it as less legitimate than other groups do, but they do not reject it entirely, considering the system to be partially meritocratic and acceptable.

  • Class 4 Against privilege (6.8%)

Urzúa, T., Castillo, J. C. (2026). Identifying Patterns of Beliefs Based on Merit and Privilege: A Latent Class Analysis Approach. Work in progress

Finally, the fourth class is the smallest with only a 7% and is characterized by a low perception of meritocracy and a low perception of the influence of privilege, specially the rich parents item. Well, the most significant aspect of this group is that it does not perceive privilege as a factor in the distribution of resources in society, nor does it believe that it should be. Based on that, the name of this class is Against privilege

Now, the next steps are to study the relationship between these classes and market justice in education based on regression models
